title: 'KARkey: A Python-based computational platform accelerating kinase biosensor engineering'
---

The human kinome, consisting of over 500 kinases, orchestrates dynamic control of the cell through phosphorylation of substrate proteins at specific cellular locations. Genetically encoded fluorescent kinase activity reporters (KARs) report specific kinase activities, serving as powerful tools to uncover the regulation and functions of these key node signaling molecules in health and disease. However, the development of KARs is limited by a one-at-a-time process, which is often trial-and-error and without global assessment of specificity against unintended kinases within the kinome. We have developed a tool called KARkey with the aim of accelerating KAR engineering. Given KAR design relies on phosphorylation of a substrate sequence by the kinase of interest and the subsequent binding to an accompanying phospho-amino acid binding domain (PAABD) to convert kinase activity to optical readout, the success of engineering new KARs hinges on the specificity and efficacy of the substrate and its compatibility with the PAABD. Using substrate specificity data for 78 tyrosine and 303 serine-threonine kinases along with data on PAABD binding specificities, KARkey algorithmically identifies candidate sensor substrates that are kinase sensitive, kinase selective, and phospho-amino acid binding domain compatible. KARkey has enhanced existing adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase (AMPK) biosensors to eliminate sensitivity towards nonspecific calcium-activated kinases. KARkey has also enabled engineering of a new sensitive and selective KAR for protein kinase G (PKG). This GKAR achieves a dynamic range of 250% ± 18% change of the signal and selectively reports PKG activity independently from several kinases, including fellow cyclic-nucleotide-activated kinase protein kinase A. Given the breadth of data forming the foundation of this tool, we anticipate KARkey will unlock new possibilities in KAR engineering to enhance existing sensors and enable KAR development for a wider range of kinases to reveal novel spatiotemporal kinase regulation.
